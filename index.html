TL;DR

EO-Jev adapts the decision model d3-flash to Earth observation. In a single forward pass, it improves significantly on 26 of 30 settings, matches or beats the specialist reference on 20 of 28, and gains 8.0 points on settings held out from adaptation.

Abstract

Earth-observation workflows often require choosing among known outcomes with a trustworthy confidence, across large volumes of imagery. Decision models return probabilities over candidate answers in a single forward pass, without autoregressive decoding, but their accuracy, calibration and transfer across EO tasks remain insufficiently understood. Our diagnostic study of d3-flash on 30 settings from 23 public EO benchmarks finds that compositional reasoning transfers well and calibration beats its Qwen3.5-9B backbone on 24 settings, while counting, change quantification, damage grading and recognising absent objects remain weak. Low-rank adaptation on public training splits gives EO-Jev which, beyond its accuracy gains, has lower calibration error than the specialist references on 21 of 28 settings, at a cost of 1.8 points of general decision performance. Quantitative perception, option-order consistency and calibration under distribution shift remain open.

Findings

Four questions, four answers

RQ1

Which EO capabilities transfer from a general-purpose decision model without EO-specific adaptation, and how does its performance compare with EO specialists?

77.5 vs 60.0LHRS-Bench: d3-flash against the best specialist

Compositional reasoning transfers and the probabilities are calibrated, but specialists lead wherever they trained on the benchmark.

  • Ahead of the best specialist on 7 of 28 settings, all of them broad reasoning (CHOICE 83.5 vs 76.9, UrBench 60.3 vs 55.6).
  • Weak on counting (33.8 on DOTA-v2), change quantity, damage grading (41.1) and abstention (30.4 on VHM deceptive).
  • Lower ECE than its own Qwen3.5-9B backbone on 24 of 30 settings.
RQ2

How does EO supervision affect performance, and do its benefits extend to datasets held out from adaptation?

+17.5points: mean gain over d3-flash across all 30 settings, in-domain (+23.8) and out-of-domain (+8.0)

Significant gains on 26 of 30 settings, and specialist level on 20 of 28 under equal supervision.

  • Correct abstention on VHM deceptive questions rises from 30.4 to 74.0 with no VHM data in adaptation.
  • fMoW country accuracy 44.4 → 70.3, with training images from before 2013 and away from every evaluation site.
  • The cost: CHOICE −1.5, LHRS-Bench strict −5.2 and 1.8 points of general decision skill.
RQ3

How do calibration and inference cost compare with the backbone and specialist baselines?

21/28settings with lower calibration error than the best specialist

Lower ECE than d3-flash on 20 of 30 settings (significant on 17) and than its backbone on 27 of 30, and a median 6.4× the throughput of the best specialist (3.2× with one question per call).

  • VHM deceptive ECE 0.254 → 0.062; xBD damage 0.168 → 0.050.
  • Specialists' probabilities are often uninformative: RSThinker reaches ECE 0.712 on NWPU VHR-10 counting.
  • Where calibration worsens, EO-Jev is over-confident (SIRI-WHU, GEOBench-VLM, fMoW continent).
RQ4

Which limitations persist after decision model adaptation to EO tasks?

90%of the specialist on NWPU VHR-10 counting, the lowest of 28 settings

Counting on unseen imagery, option-order robustness, cross-view geometry and calibration far from the training data.

  • A linear probe on d3-flash's decision state reaches 96.4 / 94.3 / 87.5 on AID / EuroSAT / NWPU, against 78.1 / 56.8 / 73.5 zero-shot: recognition was a readout problem.
  • Counting and change quantification improve less: they need new perceptual supervision.
  • UrBench orientation questions stay near chance (30.5 against a 24% baseline).
